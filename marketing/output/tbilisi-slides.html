The city smells like sulfur and woodsmoke.
The baths beneath Abanotubani have been running since the 5th century. You soak in water that comes out of the earth hot. It costs almost nothing. It feels ancient because it is.
Genadi Yakovlev / Pexels

03
Soviet concrete and medieval churches share the same block.
Tbilisi never resolved the contradiction. It just lived with it. That tension — between eras, between empires, between identities — is what makes the city feel real.
Maria Loznevaya / Pexels
4
04
Order the khinkali. Order more than you think you need.
Soup dumplings twisted at the top, filled with spiced meat and broth. You eat them with your hands. The technique takes about thirty seconds to learn and the rest of your life to forget.
Nadin Sh / Pexels

05
You will pray in a city that does not pause for you.
No call to prayer echoing through the streets. No halal sign on every corner. But the Jumah Mosque anchors the old town — Sunni and Shia under one roof — and the city holds space without making a show of it. You figure it out. You always do.
Netlenka / Pexels

06
The wine is everywhere and the grapes are 8,000 years old.
Georgia invented winemaking. They age it in clay vessels underground. You don't have to drink it to understand what it means to a people — that a culture built something this old and kept it alive. That is worth paying attention to.
Anna Romanova / Pexels
