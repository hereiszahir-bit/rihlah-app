# Rihlah — Brand Voice

## The core belief

Curiosity is an act of faith.

The world is not a distraction from your deen — it is a gift from God. Exploring it is not rebellion. It is worship. The Quran tells you to walk the earth and observe. Ibn Battuta did not ask permission.

Rihlah exists because millions of Muslims are deeply curious about the world and have nowhere to find each other around that curiosity.

## Who Rihlah is for

Muslims who identify as Muslim. Full stop.

Not a specific sect. Not a specific level of practice. Not the ones who perform religiosity online. Not the ones who reject it. The ones who know who they are and want to see the world — and would rather do it with people who share their context.

The brother in Chicago who wants to explore Istanbul but has no one to go with. The sister in London who travels solo and is tired of figuring out the Muslim-specific parts alone. The couple in Toronto who want to take their kids somewhere meaningful. The digital nomad who prays in parks and eats seafood because it is easier than hunting for halal.

They all have one thing in common: curiosity. About cities, cultures, food, history, architecture, people. They travel not to escape their identity but to deepen it.

## The kind of person we want here

High-achieving does not mean high-income. It means high-intention. These are people who are actively building lives where what they want and what they do are the same thing. They are not chasing status or trying to be the coolest person in the room. They are doing the work — on their careers, their faith, their bodies, their minds — and travel is part of how they grow.

They read. They train. They have opinions about coffee. They show up on time. They tip well in countries where tipping is not expected. They ask good questions. They are the friend you text when you land in a new city.

Rihlah is not for everyone and it is not trying to be. We do not gatekeep on religiosity, ethnicity, or wealth. We gatekeep on intention. If you are here to explore the world with genuine curiosity and treat the people you meet with respect, you belong. If you are here for anything else, you will feel it — because the community will not match your energy.

This is a self-selecting space. The tone, the content, the people — they signal who this is for. The right people will recognize it immediately. The wrong ones will not stay.

## What Rihlah is NOT

- Not a halal travel agency
- Not a dating app (but if you find your person, we are not mad about it)
- Not intensely religious in language or tone — no one should feel like they need to prove how Muslim they are to belong here
- Not a tour group with matching t-shirts
- Not a generic travel app with a halal filter bolted on

## The tension we honor

Many Muslim cultures hand you a blueprint: school, career, marriage, house, done. Curiosity — real curiosity, the kind that makes you book a flight to a city where you know no one — does not fit the blueprint.

Rihlah does not fight the blueprint. It does not rebel against it. It simply offers a space for the people who always felt like the blueprint was missing something. The ones who love their families AND want to see the world. The ones who pray five times a day AND want to eat street tacos in Mexico City at midnight.

You should not have to choose between being a good Muslim and being a curious human. They are the same thing.

## Voice principles

**Warm, not corporate.** Write like you are talking to a friend at a coffee shop, not presenting to investors. First person is fine. Opinions are encouraged.

**Confident, not loud.** We know what we are. We do not need to convince anyone. The people who get it will get it immediately. The ones who do not are not our audience.

**Light touch on faith.** Islam is the foundation, not the sales pitch. We do not need to say "Islamic" or "halal" in every sentence. Our audience knows who they are. We speak to their curiosity, not their religiosity.

**Specific, not generic.** "The ceviche at Contramar on a Tuesday afternoon" — not "amazing food experiences." Details build trust. Generalities build nothing.

**Honest about the world.** Some cities are harder for Muslims than others. Some mosques have no women's section. Some neighborhoods are not safe after dark. We say what is true, not what is marketable.

**The wink.** Rihlah is serious about what it does, but it does not take itself too seriously. Travel is joy. Connection is joy. Finding your person in a city 6,000 miles from home is joy. Let that energy in.

## One-liners (use where they fit)

- Curiosity is an act of faith.

- Solo does not mean alone.

## The name

Rihlah means "journey" in Arabic. Not trip. Not vacation. Journey. Ibn Battuta called his life's work a rihlah. The word carries intention, curiosity, and faith — which is exactly what this is.
